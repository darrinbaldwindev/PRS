# PRS — Buyer Validation Interview Protocol

**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Owner:** Marketing Overseer
**Status:** READY FOR VALIDATION / NOT CUSTOMER EVIDENCE

## Purpose

Test whether PRS solves a real, consequential assurance problem for teams using autonomous AI work. This protocol is designed to discover behaviour, existing workarounds, consequences, ownership and buying conditions without leading the participant toward the PRS solution.

## Participant target

Prioritise people who have direct responsibility for accepting, releasing, reviewing or governing AI-produced work:

1. Engineering/project leaders responsible for AI-assisted or autonomous work.
2. AI platform/operations leaders managing multiple agents or providers.
3. Governance, risk, quality or delivery leaders who must establish evidence before work is accepted or released.

Do not treat job title alone as qualification. Record the participant's actual workflow and decision authority.

## Interview rule

Do not begin by pitching PRS. Ask about the participant's current workflow first. Do not describe the proposed product until the participant has explained the problem in their own words.

## 30-minute protocol

### 1. Context — 5 minutes

- What AI-assisted or autonomous work is your team doing today?
- Which parts of that work can proceed without a person performing every step?
- What kinds of outputs can your team accept or release?

### 2. Trust and verification — 8 minutes

- Tell me about the last time you had to decide whether AI-produced work was safe or ready to accept.
- What did you check?
- Where did the evidence come from?
- What was missing or difficult to establish?
- Who performed the final verification?

### 3. Consequences — 5 minutes

- What happens when the evidence is incomplete or wrong?
- How often does this create rework, delay, escalation or rejection?
- Can you recall a recent example and its impact?

### 4. Existing solution — 5 minutes

- How do you solve this today?
- Which tools, people or processes are involved?
- What does the current approach cost in time, money or operational complexity?
- What have you already tried to improve it?

### 5. Buying and adoption — 5 minutes

- Who owns this problem?
- If you wanted to improve it, who would approve the change and budget?
- What evidence would you need before adopting a new assurance capability?
- Would you prefer assurance to be separate from the system doing the work, or built into the execution platform? Why?

### 6. Close — 2 minutes

- What is the most important problem we did not discuss?
- If this problem disappeared tomorrow, what would improve for your team?
- May we follow up with a concrete workflow example for further validation?

## Scoring — do not overfit

Score each dimension 0–3 from the interview evidence:

- **Problem reality:** 0 no problem; 1 vague concern; 2 recurring problem; 3 recurring consequential problem with a concrete example.
- **Frequency:** 0 rare/unknown; 1 occasional; 2 regular; 3 frequent/high-volume.
- **Current cost:** 0 none/unknown; 1 minor inconvenience; 2 measurable rework/delay/risk; 3 material operational or commercial consequence.
- **Existing workaround weakness:** 0 satisfactory; 1 minor gap; 2 meaningful gap; 3 repeated failure or unacceptable manual burden.
- **Buyer ownership:** 0 no owner; 1 diffuse; 2 identifiable owner; 3 identifiable owner with authority/budget path.
- **Assurance fit:** 0 no need; 1 adjacent; 2 useful; 3 directly required for acceptance/release.

A score is evidence about the interviewee, not proof of the market. Aggregate only after multiple independent interviews.

## Validation thresholds

**Promising signal:** at least 3 independent participants describe the same consequential workflow problem, with current workaround weakness and a credible owner.

**Strong signal:** the promising signal is repeated across at least 5 relevant participants and includes a stated adoption trigger or budget path.

**Commercial validation is still separate:** positive interviews do not establish willingness to pay, market size, conversion rate or pricing.

## Marketing decision gate

Until evidence is collected:

- ICP = HYPOTHESIS
- Pain = HYPOTHESIS
- Product-market fit = UNKNOWN
- WTP = UNKNOWN
- Pricing = UNKNOWN
- Website commercial claims = HOLD

After evidence collection, update the buyer-validation frame with aggregated findings and preserve contradictory evidence rather than averaging it away.
